Finding a PhD abroad

How doctoral admission works in the main systems, where funded positions are advertised, and a twelve-month plan from the first search to the first day in a new country.

A PhD abroad changes a career. It gives you new supervisors, a new network and often better funding. It also takes more planning than most students expect. The first thing to understand is that there is no single "PhD application". Each country, and sometimes each university, recruits doctoral students in its own way.

Two ways of recruiting doctoral students#

1. Position-based (like a job). A professor or a project has funding for one doctoral researcher on a defined topic. The position is advertised, you apply with a CV and a letter, and the best candidate is hired, often with an employment contract and a salary. This is common in Germany, the Netherlands, the Nordic countries, Switzerland, and in projects funded by the European Union.

2. Programme-based (like an admission). A department admits a cohort of students each year through a committee. You apply to the programme, not to a person, and you choose your supervisor later. Funding usually comes from teaching or research assistantships. This is the standard model in the United States.

Many countries mix the two. In Canada, you apply to a department, but in research-based programmes admission often depends on a professor agreeing to supervise you, so contacting supervisors early is essential. In France, doctoral contracts are awarded through competitions run by doctoral schools, usually for a topic proposed by a supervisor. In the United Kingdom, most funded places are "studentships" attached to a project or a doctoral training centre.

The main systems at a glance#

Country Typical length How you get in How it is funded
United States 5 to 6 years Apply to a department programme, deadlines usually December to January Teaching or research assistantships, fellowships
Canada 4 to 5 years Apply to a department, often with a supervisor's agreement Supervisor's grants, teaching, university and federal scholarships
United Kingdom 3 to 4 years Apply to an advertised studentship or a training centre Studentships from research councils or universities
France 3 years Apply to a thesis topic, then the doctoral school competition Doctoral contract (a salaried 3-year contract), CIFRE with a company
Germany 3 to 4 years Apply to an advertised position or a structured programme Employment contract on a project, or a scholarship
Netherlands, Nordics, Switzerland 3 to 5 years Apply to an advertised position Employment contract with a salary

The table gives typical patterns. Details vary between universities, so always check the doctoral school's own page.

Where funded positions are advertised#

  • EURAXESS (euraxess.ec.europa.eu): the European Commission's portal for research jobs, including PhD positions across Europe and positions funded by the Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions. You can filter by field, country and funding, and save searches with email alerts.
  • FindAPhD (findaphd.com) and jobs.ac.uk: the main listings for the United Kingdom, with many international positions.
  • DAAD (daad.de): the German Academic Exchange Service lists international doctoral programmes and its own scholarships. PhDGermany lists open doctoral positions.
  • ADUM (adum.fr): the portal used by many French doctoral schools to publish thesis topics and receive applications.
  • AcademicPositions (academicpositions.com): research jobs in Europe and beyond.
  • Department and research-group pages: many positions are only posted there. Follow the groups you are interested in.
  • Mailing lists and societies of your field, which often circulate PhD offers.

A twelve-month plan#

12 to 9 months before the start. Decide on two or three countries and your research area. Read recent papers in that area and list the people who wrote them. Check language requirements and test dates (for example English tests).

9 to 6 months before. Contact potential supervisors (see Contacting a professor). Prepare your CV, a statement of purpose and, where needed, a research proposal (see The PhD application file). Ask your referees early.

6 to 3 months before. Apply. Programme deadlines in the United States and Canada are often in December or January for a September start. European positions open all year. French doctoral contract competitions usually happen in spring and early summer.

3 to 0 months before. Interviews, offers, the visa, health insurance, accommodation. EURAXESS also has practical pages on moving to Europe as a researcher.

Questions to ask before accepting an offer#

  • What exactly is the funding, for how long, and what happens if the thesis takes longer?
  • Is there teaching, and how many hours?
  • Who is the supervisor, and how often will you meet?
  • Is there a doctoral school with courses, seminars and a thesis committee?
  • What is the cost of living compared with the salary or stipend?

The guide Choosing a supervisor explains how to evaluate the person you will work with for several years.

Checklist#

  • I know which recruitment model each target country uses.
  • I have a list of supervisors and groups whose recent papers I have read.
  • I have saved searches on EURAXESS and the national portals.
  • My CV, statement and proposal are ready, and my referees are informed.
  • I know the deadlines, the language requirements and the visa steps.
