Contacting a professor

How to write the first email to a potential supervisor or collaborator, what to attach, when to follow up, and the mistakes that send an email straight to the archive.

Professors receive many emails from prospective students, and most of them look the same: a long paragraph about passion, a list of grades, and a request for "any opportunity". These are rarely answered. A short, specific email that shows you have read the professor's work has a much better chance.

Before you write#

  1. Read two or three recent papers of the professor, at least the abstract and the introduction (see How to read a research paper).
  2. Check the group's page for open positions, instructions for prospective students, and the professor's current students.
  3. Check whether the professor can supervise: some are retired, on leave, or not taking students this year. The page often says so.
  4. Know the admission route of the country (see Finding a PhD abroad). In some systems, an email to a professor is essential. In others, the professor will simply tell you to apply to the programme.

The structure of a good first email#

Subject line. Make it specific: "Prospective PhD student: your work on [topic]" or "Application for the PhD position [reference]". Never "Request" or "Hello Professor".

First paragraph: who you are, in one sentence. Your current degree and university, and what you are looking for. "I am finishing a master's degree in mathematics at [University], and I am looking for a PhD position starting in September [year]."

Second paragraph: why this professor. Name one or two specific papers and say what interested you, in your own words. Connect it to what you have done: your thesis, a project, a course. This paragraph is the reason the email gets answered.

Third paragraph: what you bring. Two or three concrete facts: your master's thesis topic, a preprint, a relevant skill, a scholarship you hold or will apply for. Not a list of every course.

Fourth paragraph: the request. Ask one clear question. "Would you consider supervising a PhD student in this direction, and if so, is there an application route you would recommend?"

Attachments. A one or two-page CV in PDF and, if you have one, your master's thesis or a writing sample. Name files clearly: Lastname_CV.pdf.

An example#

Subject: Prospective PhD student: your work on [topic]

Dear Professor [Name],

I am completing a master's degree in mathematics at [University] and I am looking for a PhD position starting in September [year].

I recently read your paper "[Title]" with great interest, in particular the construction in Section 3, which relates [idea] to [idea]. My master's thesis studies [topic], and I would be very interested in understanding whether a similar approach applies to [question].

In my thesis I proved [one concrete result], and I am comfortable with [relevant tools]. I attach my CV and a draft of my thesis.

Would you consider supervising a PhD student in this direction? If so, I would be grateful for any advice on the application process.

Kind regards, [Name]

The example is a template. The point is the structure: specific, short, and honest.

Follow-up#

If there is no answer after two weeks, send one short, polite reminder in the same thread. If there is still no answer after another two weeks, move on. Silence is common and usually means the professor is busy, not that you did something wrong.

Mistakes that guarantee silence#

  • A generic email sent to dozens of professors with only the name changed. Professors notice.
  • Wrong name, wrong field, or "Dear Sir/Madam".
  • Long paragraphs about your passion with no concrete information.
  • Asking the professor to read a long proposal before any contact.
  • Attachments that are not PDF, or ten attachments.
  • Several emails to the same professor in a few days, or emails to everyone in the department at once.

After a positive answer#

A professor who answers positively may suggest a video call. Prepare it like a short interview: be ready to explain your thesis in five minutes, and prepare questions about the group (see Choosing a supervisor). Ask about funding directly and politely. It is a normal question.

Writing to a researcher about a paper#

The same principles apply when you write to an author with a question about their paper: start from their work, give a concrete example, phrase observations humbly, and ask one clear question. The guide A research method includes a model email.

Checklist#

  • I have read recent papers of the professor and checked the group's page.
  • The subject line is specific.
  • The email names a specific paper and connects it to my own work.
  • It contains one clear question.
  • My CV is attached as a short PDF with a clear file name.
  • I follow up once, after two weeks, and then move on.
